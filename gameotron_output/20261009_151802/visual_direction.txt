VISUAL DIRECTION FOR: Rule Breaker Dungeon
================================================================

PHASE 1 — FIVE VISUAL DIRECTION CONCEPTS
----------------------------------------------------------------
Each concept is scored 1–5 on Originality (O), Theme-Fit (T),
Implementability in a single canvas/CSS index.html (I), and Visual
Appeal (V). All five below include an Atmospheric Effects layer, as
required.

----------------------------------------------------------------
CONCEPT 1 — "CHALK & RUIN" (Chalkpunk Insurgent)
----------------------------------------------------------------
ART STYLE: Hand-chalked line art on a grimy slate dungeon. Every
tile, wall and glyph is drawn with a deliberately wobbly, line-boiled
chalk stroke over charcoal stone. Rules are scrawled in white chalk
and crossed out by a bolder red hand. Overlaid on top is a thin
digital glitch-static layer (scanline tears + chromatic flicker) so
the "rebellion" reads as physical chalk vandalism *and* as a system
being hacked.

PALETTE: Charcoal #1b1b22 / Slate #2b2b36 / Chalk white #f2efe4 /
Blood-scrawl red #e0453b / Shatter cyan #57e0d0 / Chalk-yellow #f2c14e

MOOD: Defiant, punk-zine energy — a dungeon vandalized by its own
prisoners. Grim but playful; the graffiti is the light source.

THEME-FIT: Very high. "Anything Goes" is literally written on the
walls; rule-banners are torn rulebook strips; every shatter cracks
the chalk layer. The theme is structural, and this style makes the
structure visible.

ATMOSPHERIC EFFECTS: chalk-dust motes drifting off freshly drawn
strokes; glitch-static wipe between rooms; 6 px screen-shake +
chromatic-aberration flash on rule-break; gravity-flip camera tilt.

----------------------------------------------------------------
CONCEPT 2 — "VHS OCCULT" (Analog Horror Dungeon)
----------------------------------------------------------------
ART STYLE: Degraded VHS/CRT dungeon. Scanlines, tracking-error
bands, phosphor bloom, timestamp overlay in the corner. Rules appear
as corrupted closed-caption text that glitches into truth. Rooms are
dim, high-contrast silhouettes lit by a single flickering source.

PALETTE: VCR black #0a0a0c / Phosphor green #7dfba0 / Magnetic
purple #a05cff / Tape-white #e8e6df / Warning red #ff2f45 / Bleed
cyan #4fd8ff

MOOD: Creepy, liminal, "found footage" dread. Rebellion as a
forbidden broadcast.

THEME-FIT: High. "Break the signal" pairs with "break the rule," and
glitch-static transitions are baked in. Slightly less playful than
the GDD's wry tone.

ATMOSPHERIC EFFECTS: rolling tracking bands, tape-jitter jitter on
shatter, phosphor flicker, VHS dropout on screen transitions,
desaturation pulse during the Bell Chamber alarm.

----------------------------------------------------------------
CONCEPT 3 — "RANSOM-ZINE" (Cut-Paper Collage)
----------------------------------------------------------------
ART STYLE: Everything is torn paper and cut-out ransom-note
typography. Tiles are cardboard/comic panels; rules are pasted
letter-clippings held with tape; the player is a paper cut-out with
visible scissor-edge. Shatter = paper tearing, not glass.

PALETTE: Kraft brown #b89a6a / Newsprint grey #d9d6c9 / Ink black
#14140f / Poster red #d6302a / Tape yellow #e8c33b / Corner blue
#3a6ea5

MOOD: Scrappy, DIY, handmade-craft rebellion. Warm and tactile.

THEME-FIT: High conceptually (torn rulebook = torn paper), but the
GDD explicitly calls for a "glass-crack shatter" signature, which
this style would fight.

ATMOSPHERIC EFFECTS: paper-grain texture, fluttering page gusts in
Torn Library, confetti of paper scraps on victory, soft drop-shadow
depth, tape-peel animations.

----------------------------------------------------------------
CONCEPT 4 — "NEON SIGIL ARCADE"
----------------------------------------------------------------
ART STYLE: Glowing vector runes on obsidian. Thin additive-blend
neon strokes, grid floors, bloom halos; rules rendered as glowing
sigils that invert to a negative on break. Very high contrast,
synthetic.

PALETTE: Obsidian #05060a / Neon magenta #ff2d95 / Electric cyan
#22e6ff / Void violet #7a3cff / Bone #f5f2ff / Ember orange #ff8a2b

MOOD: Sleek, hypnotic, arcade-futurist hedonism. "Anything goes"
as a nightclub, not a prison.

THEME-FIT: Medium. Beautiful and easy to implement, but the neon
aesthetic is generic and the dungeon/rulebook/chalk framing gets
lost — the "torn rulebook" theme becomes decoration rather than
structure.

ATMOSPHERIC EFFECTS: additive bloom, particle trails on every move,
neon ripple on tile entry, gravity-flip light-streak, alarm strobe.

----------------------------------------------------------------
CONCEPT 5 — "INK-WASH CATACOMB"
----------------------------------------------------------------
ART STYLE: Sumi-e ink wash over aged parchment. Rules brushed in
black ink; the rebellion layer is a violent slash of red. Soft
chiaroscuro walls, negative-space corridors.

PALETTE: Ink #101014 / Wash grey #3c3a3f / Parchment #e9e0cc /
Vermilion #c8392b / Moss #5a6b4f / Gold leaf #c9a227

MOOD: Ancient, solemn, melancholy. A cathedral of dead rules.

THEME-FIT: Medium. Thematically rich but tonally at odds with the
GDD's punk/playful "ANYTHING GOES" voice and its fast phone-first
arcade pacing.

ATMOSPHERIC EFFECTS: ink-in-water diffusion on shatter, drifting
dust motes, brush-stroke trails, slow vignette breathing, gold-leaf
sparkle on collectibles.

================================================================
PHASE 2 — EVALUATION
================================================================
Scores 1–5 (O = Originality, T = Theme-Fit, I = Implementability,
V = Visual Appeal). Weighted to match the pipeline (THEME-heavy,
TOUCH/feel matters).

| Concept              |  O  |  T  |  I  |  V  | Total (avg) |
|----------------------|-----|-----|-----|-----|-------------|
| 1 Chalk & Ruin       |  4  |  5  |  5  |  4  |   4.50      |
| 2 VHS Occult         |  5  |  4  |  4  |  4  |   4.25      |
| 3 Ransom-Zine        |  5  |  5  |  3  |  4  |   4.25      |
| 4 Neon Sigil Arcade  |  3  |  3  |  5  |  5  |   4.00      |
| 5 Ink-Wash Catacomb  |  4  |  3  |  3  |  4  |   3.50      |

EVALUATION NOTES:
- Concept 1 leads on Theme-Fit and Implementability simultaneously.
  Every requested motif (stark stone tiles, chalk graffiti, glass-crack
  rule shatter, glitch-static wipe, gravity-flip tilt) is natively
  drawable with canvas 2D primitives (stroke, arc, gradients, per-pixel
  noise), and its glitch overlay is a cheap post-effect.
- Concept 2 is the most original but its CRT post-processing (scanline
  shader-ish passes) costs more per-frame budget on 375 px phones and
  drifts the tone darker than the GDD's wry voice.
- Concept 3 ties Concept 1 on Theme-Fit but a paper-collage style
  contradicts the mandated "glass-crack" shatter and requires heavy
  hand-drawn typography, hurting Implementability.
- Concept 4 is the easiest to build and very pretty, but the theme
  becomes skin-deep — a fatal mismatch for a game whose entire point is
  structural rule-inversion.
- Concept 5 is atmospheric but tonally wrong and hard to keep legible
  at phone size.

SELECTED: CONCEPT 1 — "CHALK & RUIN" (Chalkpunk Insurgent).
TRANSPARENT NOTE: I selected Concept 1 because it is the only concept
that scores top-tier on Theme-Fit and Implementability at once. It fuses
the GDD's mandated physical motifs (stark stone, hand-chalked rules,
glass shatter, graffiti) with the digital glitch-static layer the GDD's
transitions and finale already call for, and every element is achievable
in vanilla canvas 2D + CSS with zero external assets. Concepts 2 and 3
were close on originality/theme but each traded away implementability or
tone; 4 and 5 lost on theme fidelity. I have augmented Concept 1 with
select strong ideas from the runners-up: VHS-style scanline jitter at
the Rule Core finale (Concept 2) and a light paper-tear accent on the
master-rulebook page (Concept 3), without importing their heavier
post-processing or palette.

================================================================
PHASE 3 — FINAL SPECIFICATION
================================================================

VISUAL DIRECTION FOR: Rule Breaker Dungeon

ART STYLE:
Hand-chalked line art on a grimy slate-and-stone dungeon, overlaid with
a thin digital glitch-static layer ("chalkpunk insurgent"). Confidence/
wobble: all strokes drawn with a 2–3 px random jitter and a slow
line-boil (stroke seed re-rolled ~6 fps) so edges vibrate like damp
chalk. Tile and wall outlines are rough, imperfect, high-contrast. Rules
are scrawled in chalk-white, then struck through by a bolder red hand.
Typography is ragged ransom-note / torn-rulebook: oversized, slightly
rotated letterforms (CSS clip-path tears + transform skew), legible at
375 px. The player token is a small defiant chalk stick-figure with a
raised marker, squash-and-stretch on each move (scaleY 1.15 → 0.9 over
90 ms ease). Overall: stark dungeon structure (stone/slate) versus a
rebellious chalk+glitch overlay — the dungeon is one half of the
palette, the vandalism the other.

COLOR PALETTE:
- Primary:   #1b1b22  (charcoal dungeon stone — dominant ground/walls)
- Secondary: #2b2b36  (slate — raised tiles, panel fills, grid depth)
- Accent 1:  #f2efe4  (chalk white — rules, tile outlines, typography)
- Accent 2:  #e0453b  (blood-scrawl red — crossed-out rules, danger,
                       Bell Chamber alarm, wrong/forbidden cues)
- Accent 3:  #57e0d0  (shatter cyan — rule-break VFX, doors unlocked,
                       gravity-flip, valid target highlight)
- Accent 4:  #f2c14e  (chalk-yellow — near-miss / score popups / zone
                       bonus; the "reward" spark)

Usage rule: stone/slate are ground; chalk white is law; red is the lie
and the danger; cyan is the break and the way forward; yellow is the
reward. Color is never the only signal — every state also carries an
icon/shape (color-plus-icon accessibility cue).

MOOD:
Defiant punk-zine energy — a dungeon vandalized by its own prisoners.
Grim but playful and wry, matching the GDD's taunting voice ("Rules are
just suggestions with good PR"). Darkness is the canvas, not the tone:
the graffiti and glitch are the light. Escalation is felt in color
temperature — rooms 1–2 cool and calm slate; rooms 3–4 cooler with
drifting motes and paper; room 5 (Bell Chamber) pulses hot red with the
alarm; room 6 (Rule Core) throbs heart-glow cyan-vs-red at the finale.

KEY ELEMENTS:
- Stone grid tiles: rough charcoal/slate quads with hand-chalked
  outlines and a subtle wobble; one deliberately "wrong" chalk tile is
  visually distinct in every room (texture + corner icon, not color
  alone).
- Player token: chalk stick-figure with raised marker; squash-and-stretch
  on move; silhouette preserved at phone size.
- RULE banner: a torn strip of the rulebook pinned across the top 12%;
  ransom-note lettering; glitches and cracks apart on break; hold-to-
  expand detail card.
- Rule-break VFX: glass-crack fracture with pivoting "rule shard"
  polygons, glyph-letter fragments, chromatic-aberration flash, 6 px
  screen-shake, hit-stop.
- Glitch-static wipe: full-screen noise/scanline band wiping between
  rooms (180 ms); menus use a 120 ms fade.
- Doors: chalk-drawn archways; dormant = dim red X, unlocked = cyan
  glow with pulsing rune arrow, mapping 1:1 to `window.WORLD.exits`.
- Graffiti layer: rebellious scrawls ("ANYTHING GOES", "RULES ARE
  LIES") revealed as rooms are cleared, chalk-white + red hand.
- Hazards: Gravity Well drifting pebbles (with dust trail); Torn
  Library wind gusts visualized as drifting pages; Bell Chamber brass
  bell with a rising red alarm ring; Rule Core chained-open master
  rulebook with throbbing heart-glow.
- HUD: rules-broken counter (0/6), things (0/4), score, room name —
  flat, oversized, phone-legible.
- Contextual controls: FLIP button and D-pad drawn as chalk buttons;
  only present in relevant rooms.

TECHNICAL NOTES (single self-contained index.html, vanilla JS):
- Rendering: Canvas 2D only, one responsive `<canvas>` scaled by
  `devicePixelRatio`, letterboxed to a fixed design aspect. All layout
  math in virtual units with a single scale factor. `requestAnimationFrame`
  with a fixed-step logic accumulator; variable render.
- Chalk line-boil: pre-render each tile/wall to an offscreen canvas at
  load; re-roll a small set (4–6) of jittered stroke variants and cycle
  them ~6 fps. Zero per-frame path re-tessellation cost.
- Glitch-static layer: a low-resolution offscreen noise buffer (e.g.
  64×64) drawn with `drawImage` nearest-neighbour and a globalAlpha
  flicker, plus a moving scanline band. Cheap, capped; only active
  during transitions and the Rule Core finale.
- Chromatic aberration: draw the scene (or just the shatter region)
  twice with red/cyan offsets at low alpha during the break flash only.
- Rule shards / particles: pooled particle array with a hard cap (e.g.
  180 live); shards are simple triangles/quads with gravity + rotation;
  popups drawn as text. Cap protects 375 px phone FPS.
- Screen tilt on gravity flip: apply a CSS/ctx transform (rotate ~8°)
  to the board container with an eased whoomp; keep HUD untransformed.
- Typography without assets: use system chalk-like stacks and CSS
  `clip-path` tears + slight `transform` skew for the ransom look —
  no webfonts, no images. Rule text sized in virtual units for 375 px.
- Touch targets ≥ 48 px; D-pad fallback bottom-left, FLIP bottom-right,
  Pause top-right; all drawn on canvas and hit-tested in virtual coords.
- Accessibility: high contrast, color-plus-icon cues on every state
  (valid tile, danger, locked door, near-miss), so color-blind players
  are never blocked; everything legible at 375 px width.
- Performance target: 60 fps at 375×667 and 1920×1080; cap particles,
  pre-render static textures, avoid per-frame allocations in the render
  loop.
- Zero external assets: no images, no webfonts, no network calls — all
  visuals are canvas primitives, gradients, and drawn typography/CSS,
  satisfying the free-to-play, fully offline requirement.

*End of Visual Direction.*
