TORRE NOSFERATU — IDEATION + EXPERT REVIEW (Phase 2)
Theme (override): "Torre Nosferatu — escape a pitch-black candle-lit tower before Count Orlok takes your light"
Reviewer: browsergame_expert (GAMEOTRON pipeline)
Scoring axes (1–5, 5 best): HOOK | TOUCH (touch-first feasibility/interaction fit) | THEME (meaningful use of candle-as-clock / descent / Dread)
Hard boundaries checked: (a) NO traditional battle system; (b) single-file vanilla-JS feasible; (c) staged delivery must carry wave 1 descent core + wave 2 social+Dread + wave 3 shared-resource survivor sim; (d) candle-as-clock, five NPCs, single 0–8 Dread dial; (e) ≥6 screens; (f) beginner first success <30s.

TOP-LINE VERDICT
=====================================================================
Highest-scoring concepts (total /15):
  14. Dread Weather ........ 14  (HOOK 5 / TOUCH 4 / THEME 5)
   7. The Cruel Candle ..... 14  (HOOK 5 / TOUCH 4 / THEME 5)
  16. Orlok's Patience ..... 14  (HOOK 5 / TOUCH 4 / THEME 5)
   1. One Candle, One Descent 14  (HOOK 4 / TOUCH 5 / THEME 5)

STRONGEST FOR A SOCIAL+DREAD-FORWARD, SINGLE-FILE BUILD:
  #14 Dread Weather is the single best concept for this brief. It makes the mandated
  0–8 Dread dial the primary escalating pressure and couples it directly to Orlok's
  closing speed and NPC wariness — exactly the wave-2 social+Dread system the brief
  requires — while still resting on the candle clock. Pick #14 as the spine.
  Pair #14 with #4 (Borrower's Lantern) or #6 (Tower of Small Mercies) for the warm pole
  and #7 (Cruel Candle) for the cold pole: together they cover both disposition
  directions and the three NPC/social waves without bolt-on scope.
  #1 (One Candle, One Descent) is the safest *implementation* anchor — the cleanest
  candle/wax/turn loop — but its social/Dread layer is thin, so it is best used as the
  wave-1 core underneath a social pick rather than as the whole concept.
  #19 (Warm Hand) is the strongest single wave-3 (shared-resource sim) expression.

BOUNDARY VIOLATIONS: none literal. No concept proposes an HP/attack battle system;
all encounters are framed as Run/Endure. Flagged risks (not violations):
  - #11 Blind Leap: the word "platforming" risks drifting to a reflex/jump system,
    which the brief forbids (grid-adjacent recon hops only, never twitch). Would need
    explicit re-framing to stay compliant.
  - #2 The Guttering Metronome: defining hook is audio-only ("the metronome is the
    clock"). Feasible in WebAudio, but the hook is invisible and undemonstrable in a
    store thumbnail — high jam-scroll risk.
  - #3, #5, #8, #9, #10, #11, #13, #17, #18, #20: solo/descent-forward. They do not
    violate a boundary, but none foregrounds the *mandatory* five-NPC + Dread wave-2
    system. Each would require the pipeline to bolt on social play — coverage risk,
    not a rule break.
  - #20 Nosferatu's Shadow: "mastering a widening bestiary" is one framing choice away
    from reading as combat. Keep strictly to Run/Endure.
OVERALL READ: A strong concept set that honors the candle-as-clock spine. The set is
top-heavy with pure-descent concepts (#1, #3, #5, #13, #17, #18) that are elegant but
under-serve the required social+Dread waves; the social concepts (#4, #6, #7, #12, #14,
#15, #19) are where the brief's mandatory scope actually lives. Recommend #14 as spine,
supported by #4/#7/#19.

=====================================================================
CONCEPT-BY-CONCEPT REVIEW
=====================================================================

1. One Candle, One Descent
A faithful, beginner-first turn-based grid roguelike: wake at the Spire with one candle and work
floor by floor down toward the front door, tapping adjacent tiles to move through total black.
Built for first-time and horror-curious players who want dread you can think your way out of.
DEFINING HOOK: the purest expression of the brief — one turn, one burn, one shrinking radius,
and the map only exists where you have already spent light. CANDLE/WAX/LOSS LOOP: every move
burns wax and shrinks the reveal radius; wax rooms refill it; zero wax gutters into Orlok's
terminal grace, where one last match or the exit can still save you.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 4  TOUCH: 5  THEME: 5
RATIONALE: Best-in-set touch fit (tap-adjacent-tile is natively finger-sized) and a near-perfect
theme execution of candle-as-clock-as-map. HOOK is 4 not 5 because "purest expression of the
brief" is faithful rather than surprising — it lacks an ownable twist for a thumbnail. Social/
Dread layer is thin, so as a *whole* it under-serves wave 2; treat it as the wave-1 core.

2. The Guttering Metronome
A turn-based game whose turns are punctuated by an audible slow tick that quickens imperceptibly
as wax drops, so the player feels the cost before reading the meter. Aimed at rhythm-sensitive
players who like tension delivered through sound. DEFINING HOOK: the wax clock is literally the
music — the metronome is the clock, and killing the tick is the zero-wax cue. CANDLE/WAX/LOSS
LOOP: each tick-bounded turn spends wax and reshapes the tempo; matches silence a snuff but the
metronome never stops; at zero the tick collapses into a single heartbeat.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 3  TOUCH: 4  THEME: 4
RATIONALE: The audio-clock is a beautiful match to audio direction §12 (the burning tick), and
touch is unaffected. But the *defining* hook is invisible — it cannot be shown in a screenshot
or a jam scroll, so its HOOK score suffers. Single-file WebAudio is feasible; this is best used
as a sound-flourish layer on another concept, not as the concept itself. No social/Dread.

3. Wax Constellations
A turn-based explorer where every tile you have lit stays faintly remembered as a dim afterimage,
so your own consumption draws a private map across the dark. For players who love fog-of-war and
self-authored cartography. DEFINING HOOK: the map is a scar of everything you burned — you
navigate by the ghost of your own past light. CANDLE/WAX/LOSS LOOP: revealing new tiles costs
wax; the remembered map is free to re-cross but never refills light; wax-out dims the entire
constellation at once.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 4  TOUCH: 4  THEME: 4
RATIONALE: "Map as a scar of spent light" is an elegant, ownable identity with genuine theme
resonance. Touch is straightforward (tap-move). Weaker than #1 on theme because the afterimage
mechanic slightly loosens the pure wax→radius coupling, and there is no social/Dread presence.

4. The Borrower's Lantern
A social-first descent where the central verb is sharing and borrowing light from the five
survivors rather than hoarding it. For players who prefer relationships and negotiation to
looting. DEFINING HOOK: warmth compounds — an ally's lantern widens your radius and can split
the drain, so trust is a resource with real geometry. CANDLE/WAX/LOSS LOOP: sharing wax now
shrinks your own light but buys a sustained shared radius; a broken alliance collapses the
lantern; wax-out with an ally present is a lesser, borrowed ending.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 4  TOUCH: 4  THEME: 5
RATIONALE: The strongest social-first expression — "trust as geometry" directly instantiates the
warm/allied shared radius the brief describes. Social verbs map cleanly to tap-based UI panels.
The only gap is wave 1: it assumes the descent core rather than defining it, so it wants a
descent anchor beneath it. Excellent partner for #14.

5. The Long Snuff
A snuff-and-relight survival game that makes darkness recoverable and matches precious, turning
each extinguishing into a mini-crisis rather than a death. For players who want loss without
instant failure. DEFINING HOOK: snuff is not death — crossing blind between one struck match
and the next is the whole game. CANDLE/WAX/LOSS LOOP: a snuff blackouts you until a match
relights the candle; matches do not restore wax, so relights only buy time; wax-out, not the
snuff, is the only true end.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 4  TOUCH: 4  THEME: 5
RATIONALE: Crisp, high-clarity hook that makes the brief's snuff-vs-wax-out distinction the
entire game — strong theme. Beginner-friendly and low-risk to build. Touch-neutral. No social/
Dread, so it is a wave-1 core or a mechanic to graft, not a full submission concept.

6. A Tower of Small Mercies
A warm-run social concept built entirely around mercy — aid, atonement, and the Dread dial
falling as you help strangers down the stairs. For players who like playing the good guy in a
cruel world. DEFINING HOOK: Dread reads as weather that visibly clears as you act warmly, and a
low-Dread tower is a demonstrably safer tower. CANDLE/WAX/LOSS LOOP: warm acts cost wax now and
return shared light later; a cold emergency can always be clawed back with gestures; wax-out
with a clean heart means a stranger may hold the door for you.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 4  TOUCH: 4  THEME: 5
RATIONALE: One of the best Dread-executions: Dread as clearing weather is exactly the brief's
"weather" framing. Fully covers wave 2 and the borrowed-door ending. Risk: if warm acts are
always claw-back-able it can flatten tension — the cold pole must remain live. Strong partner
for #7.

7. The Cruel Candle
A cold-run counterpoint built on theft, threats, and extracted light, where cruelty buys tempo
and Dread buys Orlok's speed. For players who enjoy villain-protagonist economics. DEFINING
HOOK: cruelty is a real, efficient strategy — right up until the accelerated Orlok collects.
CANDLE/WAX/LOSS LOOP: steals and threats return instant wax and matches and raise Dread; higher
Dread tightens Orlok's search, so the wax you steal is spent running; a cold run reaches wax-out
sooner and the guttering dark is shorter.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 5  TOUCH: 4  THEME: 5
RATIONALE: The tightest danger-loop in the set: cold acts buy light *now* and buy Dread *later*,
so every theft self-finances its own pursuit. That is a genuinely gripping, ownable hook.
Touch is panel-driven, fine. It is one pole (cold) — best as the cold spine paired with a warm
concept, per the brief's warm↔cold balance.

8. Portrait of a Burning Man
A visual-signature descent where ancestral portraits line the walls and their painted eyes track
your flame across the room. For art-forward players who want Expressionist horror as the main
attraction. DEFINING HOOK: the tower is watching — portraits orient toward the light, so
silhouette and composition carry the fear, not combat. CANDLE/WAX/LOSS LOOP: each tile of
movement slides the gaze and burns wax; a room fully "seen" by portraits may darken early;
wax-out makes every painted eye turn to Orlok's approach.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 4  TOUCH: 3  THEME: 4
RATIONALE: Gorgeous signature and a real fit for the visual direction's tracking-eye motif. But
TOUCH is docked: painting/animating many tracking portraits while keeping every threat
identifiable from silhouette at 375 px is a heavy single-file render load and a readability risk
at the radius edge. No social/Dread. Best as a visual layer, not a whole concept.

9. Rats in the Wainscot
A vermin-aware descent where rats are attracted to light and food and gnaw away your matches
before you have spent them. For players who like attrition and inventory dread. DEFINING HOOK:
the threat to your light is not the flame going out but your means of relighting it being eaten.
CANDLE/WAX/LOSS LOOP: rats destroy matches on contact, converting a healthy candle into a
one-snuff-away candle; wax burns per turn as normal; losing your last match while wax is low is
the real, silent countdown.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 4  TOUCH: 4  THEME: 4
RATIONALE: A smart inversion — attacking the relight economy rather than the flame. But it
elevates one creature (rats, already in the brief's roster) into the whole game, so it narrows
rather than widens the design. No social/Dread. Good creature mechanic; weak as a concept spine.

10. The Stair That Skips
A routing-and-rhythm descent built on winding, non-stacked floor graphs where some stairs jump a
tier and the fast way down is rarely the safe way. For players who love spatial puzzles and
optimal-route planning. DEFINING HOOK: "anything goes" geometry — the tower is a lawless graph,
and choosing your stair is your single biggest wax decision. CANDLE/WAX/LOSS LOOP: the short
stair saves turns and wax but risks a camped survivor or a creature room; the wax-rich stair is
longer; every reroute re-burns light, and dead ends are pure lost wax.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 4  TOUCH: 4  THEME: 4
RATIONALE: Directly channels the brief's "lawless / anything goes" geometry and makes routing
the core wax decision — a clean, ownable identity. Notably it already reserves room for social
stakes ("a camped survivor"), so it invites the wave-2 layer. Touch is fine. Conceptually strong
but abstract for a jam thumbnail; best folded into a social spine.

11. Blind Leap
A recon-first platforming concept where the next foothold is only visible inside the radius, so
every jump is scouted before it is taken. For players who like deliberate, turn-based spatial
puzzles. DEFINING HOOK: platforming with the lights on your own schedule — the jump is never a
reflex test, it is a reconnaissance problem. CANDLE/WAX/LOSS LOOP: scouting the landing costs a
turn and wax; committing blind is a gamble that can spill wax or snuff the candle; a missed
landing is never death, only a costly re-route.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 3  TOUCH: 3  THEME: 3
RATIONALE: Lowest-scoring concept. The brief permits only "simple platforming … grid-adjacent,
one-tile moves," but a concept whose entire identity is platforming invites scope drift toward a
reflex/jump system the brief forbids (BOUNDARY RISK — needs explicit re-framing to stay
compliant). Touch control of a jump verb is also the least finger-friendly of the set. Not
recommended as a spine.

12. A Debt of Light
A social-economy descent where lore fragments are the only real currency and knowledge, not wax,
is what you trade. For players who like dialogue, secrets, and non-violent progression.
DEFINING HOOK: a fragment is non-rival knowledge — you can offer the same torn page to every
survivor without ever losing it — so standing, not stock, is the wealth. CANDLE/WAX/LOSS LOOP:
lore buys disposition, hints (saved exploration = saved wax), and safe passage, NEVER wax or
matches; so clever trading stretches light indirectly; a player who trades nothing descends on
wax alone.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 4  TOUCH: 4  THEME: 4
RATIONALE: Faithfully implements the brief's non-rival lore economy and the "knowledge, never
light" rule — a rigorous, on-spec social concept. Slightly abstract (a "wealth of standing" is
harder to feel moment-to-moment than a lantern), and it leans on wave 2 heavily, so it needs a
descent core. Solid social leg for a #14 spine.

13. The Six-Minute Tower
A pacing-forward descent tuned around an explicit ~5–8 minute, ~75–100 turn run, with a visible
turn/wax budget and a speedrun-friendly score. For players who like tight, replayable time
trials. DEFINING HOOK: the candle is a stopwatch you can watch expire — the run is a single
well-budgeted arc, not a sprawling crawl. CANDLE/WAX/LOSS LOOP: ~1 burn per turn against ~30
wax with one guaranteed refill room per floor; efficiency is everything; wax-out arrives exactly
when a sloppy route meets an unspent refill.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 4  TOUCH: 4  THEME: 4
RATIONALE: Turns the brief's stated run budget into the headline hook — strong pacing discipline
and directly supports the <30s first-success rule. But it is a *pacing* concept, not a fantasy:
it foregrounds neither the social wave nor Dread, so as a full submission it under-covers the
mandatory scope. Useful as a tuning lens on the eventual spine.

14. Dread Weather
A Dread-dial-forward concept where the tower-wide 0–8 heat dial is the primary escalating
pressure and NPC wariness is the first thing you feel. For players who like systemic,
emergent-difficulty design. DEFINING HOOK: Dread is authored as weather — it colours the whole
frame, starts strangers wary, and sets how fast Orlok closes during the guttering dark. CANDLE/
WAX/LOSS LOOP: cold acts buy light and raise Dread; warm acts lower Dread and cost light; at
wax-out, the Dread tier literally decides your remaining grace, so social choices are survival
choices.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 5  TOUCH: 4  THEME: 5
RATIONALE: The single most on-brief concept. It makes the mandated 0–8 Dread dial the primary
pressure, ties it to Orlok's closing speed and to NPC wariness, and makes social choices
*identical to* survival choices — the exact wave-2 social+Dread system the brief requires, with
a frame-colouring "weather" presentation that is visually ownable and screenshot-able. Touch is
panel/tile driven. RECOMMENDED SPINE for a social+Dread-forward build.

15. The Door Ajar
A race-to-the-exit concept built around the borrowed-door ending, where a survivor might reach
the front door first and leave it open only briefly, depending on disposition and Dread. For
players who like chasing a moving window and dynamic endings. DEFINING HOOK: you can win by
someone else's grace — a warm ally at low Dread holds the ajar door longer, but while it stands
open Dread rises and creatures gather at the exit. CANDLE/WAX/LOSS LOOP: a borrowed exit costs a
reduced score and is a gamble against rising Dread; the key on your own light is the reliable
path; wax-out during the dash to a borrowed door is the cruelest loss.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 4  TOUCH: 4  THEME: 5
RATIONALE: Implements the brief's borrowed-door ending with real mechanical teeth (a moving
window bounded by disposition and Dread, with an escalating cost while it stands open). A
beautifully thematic climax, but it is an *ending* concept — it needs a full descent + social
body beneath it. Ideal as the win-condition layer on #14.

16. Orlok's Patience
A fail-forward descent that makes the guttering dark its own authored act rather than a game
over screen, with shrinking radius and closing footsteps as a final micro-game. For players who
want dramatic last stands. DEFINING HOOK: zero wax does not end the run — it begins the
guttering dark, a slow-motion scramble where a match or the exit can still be reached. CANDLE/
WAX/LOSS LOOP: the guttering dark gives a few turns of shrinking light and closing Orlok; Dread
sets how fast he closes; spending the last grace heedlessly kills, and only then does Orlok take
you.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 5  TOUCH: 4  THEME: 5
RATIONALE: The richest treatment of the brief's fail-forward terminal state — it makes the
guttering dark the emotional climax the direction explicitly asks for, and wires Dread into it.
Touch-neutral. Social content is light (Dread is present, the five NPCs are not), so it is a
magnificent terminal layer rather than a full spine.

17. Candle Math
A puzzle-forward descent where locks, levers, collapsing floors, and light-sensitive glyphs make
each floor a turn-and-light optimisation problem. For players who want calm, decision-driven
horror without dexterity. DEFINING HOOK: every obstacle is a routing puzzle priced in wax — a
wrong answer costs light, never a life. CANDLE/WAX/LOSS LOOP: solving by the long-safe path
trades turns for safety and spends wax; the snuff-cross-a-glyph trick spends a match instead;
wax-out during a half-solved floor is the fail state.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 3  TOUCH: 4  THEME: 4
RATIONALE: Faithful to the brief's puzzle guidance (§6.4) and keeps everything turn-based and
touch-safe. But puzzle-forward is calm by design, so its scroll-stopping HOOK is low, and a
puzzle-rich concept is the heaviest single-file content lift in the set. No social/Dread.
Best as floor-flavor within a larger spine.

18. The Sconce Census
A memory-and-documentation concept where the player is implicitly mapping the tower's iron
sconces and known rooms, reading the dark more than the geometry. For players who like quiet,
observational horror. DEFINING HOOK: the game rewards remembering where light lives — sconces,
refills, and safe rooms you have already found become a mental asset you exploit turn after
turn. CANDLE/WAX/LOSS LOOP: every re-exploration of forgotten ground is wasted wax; census-savvy
routing stretches each candle; incomplete recollection means backtracking burns the clock.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 3  TOUCH: 4  THEME: 4
RATIONALE: A cerebral, theme-true memory concept (rewarding recall of where light lives), but
quiet and low-spectacle — the weakest hook for a scrolling jam viewer. No social/Dread. Valuable
as a skill layer, not a spine.

19. A Warm Hand in the Dark
A companion-centric descent where Petr — the innocent worth protecting — is a live escort whose
presence both widens your light and slows your pace. For players who like escort tension and
emotional stakes. DEFINING HOOK: protecting someone costs tempo and wax now but pays in shared
light and a warmer tower, making the "protect" choice a genuine economic trade. CANDLE/WAX/LOSS
LOOP: the companion splits the drain and widens the radius while alive; creatures and traps can
harm the companion, collapsing your light; abandoning them for speed is the cold path, and
wax-out with them beside you is the warmest, worst ending.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 4  TOUCH: 4  THEME: 5
RATIONALE: The best expression of the brief's *wave-3* shared-resource sim: an NPC that carries
light, can be harmed by creatures/traps, and whose loss collapses your radius is precisely the
survivor-sim the must-haves demand. Emotional stakes are high and the "protect vs. abandon"
trade is genuine. Singular-NPC focus means it covers one survivor deeply; pair with a
five-NPC/Dread spine.

20. Nosferatu's Shadow
A creature-awareness descent where the escalating roster (bats, rats, spiders, wolves, shades,
the light-eater) is the descending player's real curriculum, introduced one per floor. For
players who like learning and mastering a widening bestiary through avoidance. DEFINING HOOK:
each floor teaches exactly one new creature and then combines types, so the dark itself becomes
a readable, masterable language. CANDLE/WAX/LOSS LOOP: every creature costs light — Run spills
wax, Endure spills wax and snuffs the candle (needing a match); deeper density means the same
wax must survive more spills; wax-out triggers Orlok, who is never part of the roster.
--- REVIEW ---
HOOK: 4  TOUCH: 4  THEME: 5
RATIONALE: Excellent one-variable-per-floor laddering, exactly matching the brief's escalation
rule, and it correctly keeps Orlok out of the normal roster. Theme is strong. Caution: "mastering
a bestiary" must stay strictly Run/Endure to avoid reading as a combat system (near-boundary —
watch the framing). No social/Dread.
